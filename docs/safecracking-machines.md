# Machines named in published safe-lock sources

This note lists named devices from pages fetched on 2026-10-02. For each
device it records only the name the page uses, the quantity that page
says the device measures, and the source URL. It is not a procedure for
opening a safe, and it does not add device names or numbers that those
pages do not state.

## Soft Drill

Matt Blaze, *Safecracking for the computer scientist* (draft 7 December
2004, revised 21 December 2004), footnote 7:

https://www.mattblaze.org/papers/safelocks.pdf

Blaze names a commercial product, the Soft Drill. He writes that it uses
a laptop computer to control a servo and a transducer, and that it is
said to perform manipulation attacks against most Group 2 locks. The
footnote does not name a measured quantity for the transducer. This note
does not supply one.

## Dial-reading aids in the same paper

Same URL, section 3.3.2. These are reading aids, not powered machines.
Blaze does not give the first two a product name.

- A sticker that fits around the dial ring, marked with a 1/4 graduation
  scale (his Figure 15). It is a scale for reading dial location.
- A Vernier scale that fits to the dial and ring. Blaze writes that it
  makes it easy to read the dial to a precision of 1/8 of a graduation.
  He says it was no longer commercially marketed when he wrote.
- Amplifiers with magnetically attached transducers, sold by safe and
  vault supply houses. Blaze does not give them a product name. He writes
  that contact points are most reliably identified by feel rather than by
  sound. The page does not state a numeric measurement for these
  amplifiers.

The same section describes how a person reads contact points by hand.
That description is not repeated here.

## Lock combination decoder (US 4,905,490)

Glenn E. Wilson, US patent 4,905,490, "Lock combination decoder" (filed
25 August 1988, patent date 6 March 1990):

https://patents.google.com/patent/US4905490A/en

The abstract and the description name these measuring parts:

- A rotary encoder. The abstract says a preferred resolution of 1000
  PPR, and a resolution of 0.09 degrees of rotation using quadrature,
  which permits the dial to be rotated accurately to within 1/10 of a
  digit. The description says the encoder indicates the position of the
  dial and provides a position feedback output and a velocity feedback
  output for rotary position and rotary speed. It also says a preferred
  encoder is two-phase, with a resolution of substantially one thousand
  parts per rotation, which it equates to increments of 1/10 of a digit
  on a one-hundred digit dial.
- A sound transducer, described as a highly sensitive microphone or
  similar, mounted so that it is in audible contact with the lock. The
  patent says this provides an indication when the contact point is
  reached.

The patent also describes a manipulation program. This note does not
restate that program, its step sizes, or its worked examples.

## Lock decoder (US 4,433,563)

Glenn E. Wilson, US patent 4,433,563, "Lock decoder" (filed 7 April 1982,
patent date 28 February 1984):

https://patents.google.com/patent/US4433563A/en

Measuring parts the patent names:

- A single-pole, one-hundred-throw switch, with a wiper and one hundred
  contacts, wired to an encoder board. The patent says each contact
  represents a dial number from 0 through 99, and that the encoder output
  indicates the number indexed under the dial indicator. The stepping
  motor is arranged to step through 100 increments per revolution, and
  each increment is one integer on the dial face.
- A pressure-sensitive switch under the stepping-motor mount. The patent
  says the switch stays open while the motor turns freely, and that it
  closes when the mount deflects because torque rises after the shaft is
  restricted from further movement.

The patent also describes an ordered dialing sequence. This note does
not restate that sequence.

## What this does not do

Nothing here is a set of steps, settings, or a procedure for opening a
mounted safe or any other container. No museum instrument is listed,
because this note only includes devices from the three URLs above.
