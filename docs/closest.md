# Closest to a breakthrough? (ranked, hype-checked)

Rankings are about **tractability for incremental progress**, not a promise that decipherment is imminent.

## Tier A — Real cracks keep happening (historical ciphers)

1. **DECODE / DECRYPT historical ciphertexts**  
   Thousands of digitized ciphertexts and keys; CrypTool + transcription tools; active HistoCrypt community.  
   **Why close:** Known European languages, known cipher families (homophonic, nomenclators), growing labeled data.  
   **Hype check:** Many items are hard (nomenclators, short texts, damaged images)—but *solvable in principle*.  
   **URLs:** https://de-crypt.org/ · https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/

2. **Remaining German Army / Enigma hobby messages (Crypto Cellar)**  
   Public transcripts; recent AI-assisted and human breaks (e.g. MVUEH Sept 2026, validated by Weierud).  
   **Why close:** Machine model known; cribs from sibling messages; small search spaces relative to ancient scripts.  
   **Hype check:** Remaining messages are *the hard residue* (short, garbled). Not “press button, win.”  
   **URLs:** https://cryptocellar.org/bgac/ · https://cryptocellar.org/bgac/1941-msg-list-unbroken.html · https://www.cryptocellar.org/bgac/the-mvueh-break.html

3. **Early-modern diplomatic letters with sibling keys**  
   When a key or sister plaintext exists in the same archive volume, breaks look like Copiale / Mary Stuart letter recoveries.  
   **Hype check:** Without key or crib, nomenclators remain brutal.

## Tier B — Partial script knowledge, language still hard

4. **Linear A (via Linear B values)**  
   Sign values often assignable; administrative structure visible; language unknown.  
   **Why “close” relative to Phaistos:** Digitized corpora (SigLA), clear genre.  
   **Hype check:** Without bilinguals or a known language family match that convinces specialists, “readings” stay provisional.  
   **URL:** https://phis.me/sigla/

5. **Etruscan / Iberian (readable scripts)**  
   Progress is lexicography and new inscriptions—not cipher-breaking.  
   **Hype check:** Do not market as “undeciphered codes.”

6. **Meroitic**  
   Script known since Griffith; language incomplete. Specialist linguistics, not amateur substitution solving.

7. **Cypro-Minoan signary consolidation**  
   Computational paleography (e.g. 2022 PLOS One unsupervised work) may stabilize the inventory—necessary *before* decipherment.  
   **Hype check:** Stabilizing signs ≠ reading the language.  
   **URL:** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0269544

## Tier C — Large interest, structural blockers

8. **Rongorongo** — Growing digital transliterations; still no accepted decipherment.  
9. **Indus script** — Huge public interest; brevity + no bilingual dominate. Treat new “decipherments” as unverified until specialist consensus.  
10. **Voynich** — Best digitized mystery book; statistical dead-ends repeatedly. Yale hosts images, not solutions.  
11. **Rohonc** — Active Hungarian scholarship; competing models; not settled.  
12. **Isthmian / Epi-Olmec** — Contested partial decipherments; corpus too small for uniqueness.

## Tier D — Effectively blocked without new finds

13. **Phaistos Disc** — Single text.  
14. **Cascajal** — Single long text.  
15. **Byblos script** — Handful of inscriptions.  
16. **Neolithic proto-writing (Vinča, Tărtăria, Dispilio)** — May not be language writing at all.

## Practical takeaway

If the goal is **a motivated individual making progress**, stay in **Tier A**. Use Tiers B–C for long-horizon study and careful negative results. Skip Tier D for “solve” ambitions.
