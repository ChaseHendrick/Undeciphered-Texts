# Check digits, and the tall line

4 October 2026. Not a reading. No letter string is stored.

Seven check-digit rules were written down before the shuffles. They are the ordinary clerical ones: one digit of a printed group of five equals the sum of the others mod 10, the whole group sums to 0 mod 9 or mod 10, or a digit is copied onto the next group. The best single rule is the first digit equaling the sum of the other four. It holds for 11 of the 79 groups. 156 of 2,000 pair shuffles do as well. That is 7.8 percent. Letting each shuffle keep its own best rule of the seven, 742 of 2,000 match the real best. Neither number is under 5 percent. There is no check digit.

The other count is the one already noticed in the fourth column: one symbol sits there six times. Across every column, 357 of 10,000 shuffles reach six. That, by itself, is under 5 percent. The same question was then asked of the rows. The tallest row holds one symbol five times, which 3,238 of 10,000 shuffles reach. Taking the taller of the row and the column, 720 of 10,000 shuffles reach six. That is 7.2 percent. The six was a column fact only until a row was allowed to count. It does not clear the bar.
