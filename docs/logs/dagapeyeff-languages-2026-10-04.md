# What if it is not English

4 October 2026. 30,000 draws. No letter string is stored.

The 25-cell square puts I and J in one cell for every Latin-alphabet language. That is the grid he printed, not a choice of English. Each language is then compared with text drawn from its own letter rates. The book's solved English exercise is the check: the same function accepts it as English (score 4.16, and 1,598 of 2,000 English draws are at least that flat) and rejects it as Italian (0 of 2,000).

Rates for French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, and Dutch are the percentages already cited from the Wikipedia letter-frequency table. Esperanto is Ottó Haszpra's table, as printed on the Esperanto Wikipedia page "Literofteco". The six hatted letters are folded into c, g, h, j, s, and u, leaving 22 letters. Latin and a Russian transliteration were not scored: no table of that form was in hand, and none was invented.

French is the least bad fit, and it is still the far edge of French: 2 draws out of 2,000. German is worse than English. Dropping the English assumption does not produce a language.

An earlier pass let every language shop for a merged pair and picked Italian. That Italian rule does not survive contact with the solved English sentence, and Italian text of the cipher's length still lands on the cipher's score only 6 times in 2,000.
