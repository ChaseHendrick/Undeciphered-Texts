# The aligned runs are not the only copies in their rows

5 October 2026. 40,000 shuffles. No letter string is stored.

The two runs of three were already scored. Both start in column 10. The cell of each run also appears twice more in that same row, outside the run.

The run of 75 in row 2 also sits at columns 1 and 6. The run of 63 in row 6 also sits at columns 0 and 6. The score is the count of those outside copies, not which columns they occupy.

The alignment alone is not the claim. Of 40,000 shuffles, 866 already have two runs in one starting column. Only 14 of those 866 also give every such run two outside copies. That is 1.62 percent. The extra copies still clear 5 percent after the alignment is given.

A vertical run is not this question. The solved exercise is not a 14 by 14 grid, so it is not the control. The extra copies are not a reading, and they do not name a letter.
