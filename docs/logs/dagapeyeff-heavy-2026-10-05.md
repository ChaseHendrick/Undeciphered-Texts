# A full row with an empty cell

5 October 2026. Not a reading. No letter string is stored.

This is about which two digits share a cell, not about the order of the cells. The row of digit 6 has 56 entries and still has an empty cell. The row of digit 8 has 80 entries and none. The exact chance that the row of 56 has an empty cell, keeping both digit totals, is rarer than 1 in 37005. The chance for the row of 80 is about 1 in 20 million. Adding those two chances stays under 5 percent.

The same rule on the columns does not. The fullest column that still has an empty cell has 46 entries, and the chance of that is 76.7 percent. A row that has only one entry forces an empty cell into four of the five columns, so a heavy column with an empty cell is the usual case. The two directions together do not clear 5 percent.

The solved exercise's fullest row with an empty cell has 26 entries. That chance is under 1 percent. The exercise clears the bar on its rows. The challenge does not, once columns are counted the same way.
