This is about which two digits share a cell, not about the order of the cells. The row whose first digit is 9 has five counts, one for each column digit in the order 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. The counts are 12, 3, 2, 1, and 0. Each step is smaller than the one before it. No other row does this, and no column does.

The comparison keeps both digit totals and re-pairs them. 1,432 of 100,000 re-pairings have a row that steps this way, allowing either direction. That is 1.43 percent.
