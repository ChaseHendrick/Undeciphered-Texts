# Repeating shifts, and why that is not a solution

4 October 2026. 666,450 key evaluations. No letter string is stored.

Moving the 196 cells around cannot fix the letter counts. Subtracting a repeating shift inside the 5 by 5 square can, because each phase of the repeat is rotated by a different amount. That is the classical next step after a plain Polybius square: a short numeric key added to every cell.

Every one of the 625 period-2 keys was scored. The friendliest letter-count score is 6.43. Eleven of 40 shuffled copies of the cipher, given the same free choice of key, do that well or better.

Every one of the 15,625 period-3 keys was scored. The friendliest score is 2.80, which is inside the range of ordinary English counts. One of 40 shuffled copies reaches it too. The search is large enough to paint English-looking counts onto text that has been stirred.

The word score is the part that would have to rise if this were a reading. On the friendliest period-3 key it is -3.5039 per letter. Letters drawn one at a time from English frequencies sit at -3.5864. The book's own solved exercise, real prose, sits at -2.5185. Five of the 40 shuffled ciphers, after their own best key, score at least as well as the real cipher. The order of the cells is not English.

So the model that can change the counts was searched completely for the two shortest repeats, and it fails the control. This is not a solution.
