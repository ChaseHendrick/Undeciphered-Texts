# Rails, diagonals, and the every-other-cell trap

4 October 2026. 20,000 shuffled deletions, plus the rails and diagonals. No letter string is stored.

Renaming symbols cannot add dependence, and column keys did not find a prose-like order. The next routes are a rail fence, three diagonals, and deleting every kth cell.

The best rail is period 3, score 0.7017. Reading straight down the 14 columns, which is a rail of period 14, scores 0.6684, the same number as before. The best diagonal tried, step (1, -1), scores 0.6582. None of these is near same-length prose.

Deleting every other cell is the one that looks like a hit. Phase 0 keeps 98 cells and scores 1.0556. Same-length English, 196 letters, had scored 1.0658, so the two numbers sit next to each other. They are not the same test. English of 98 letters scores 1.2922, and German of 98 letters scores 1.5715. A shorter text scores higher. The deletion never reaches prose of its own length.

It also does not beat a shuffled cipher. 963 of 10,000 shuffles, after the same every-other-cell deletion, score at least 1.0556. If each shuffle is allowed to pick its better phase, 1,930 of 10,000 do. The rise is what happens when the text gets shorter.

Packing every repeated cell into one block scores 2.4135, higher than prose. A high score is not a reading. That order is just the repeats stacked.

No letter string is stored.
