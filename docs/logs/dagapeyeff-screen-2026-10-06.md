# Which languages could give the cells' letter counts, 6 October 2026

Not a reading. No letter string is stored.

A one-to-one letter key, with or without any transposition, keeps letter counts. `engine.dagapeyeff_screen` asks, for 39 languages, how many single-cell errors it would take at the least to turn a 196-letter window of real text into the cells' counts, under any relabeling ([errors](dagapeyeff-errors-2026-10-06.md) explains the count). The texts are the sentences of [Universal Dependencies](https://github.com/UniversalDependencies) treebanks, fetched into the ignored `work/` folder; only counts are stored. Accents are removed, a few letters are spelled out (ß as SS, þ as TH, ł as L), Russian is transliterated in a plain British style, and J is folded into I as in the book's square. Albanian and Tagalog had under 20,000 letters and were left out. Treebank sentences are mixed genres.

The remaining languages need 10 to 27. English needs 8 at its closest corpus window and about 28 at the median ([corpus](dagapeyeff-corpus-2026-10-05.md)), Manzoni's Italian 9 ([Italian](dagapeyeff-italian-2026-10-06.md)).

Latin is the clear outlier: the lowest median of all, and hundreds of windows within the eight or so errors the book's own exercise suggests. It uses few letters (no K, W or Y) and spreads its common letters evenly, which is what the cells look like. That is a reason to search Latin, not a reading.
