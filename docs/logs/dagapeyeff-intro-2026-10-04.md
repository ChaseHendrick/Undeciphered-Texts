# The wait for a new cell

4 October 2026. Not a reading. No letter string is stored.

The question is how long the text goes without using a cell it has not used before. The longest such wait in the challenge is 22 cells. 304 of 10,000 pair shuffles wait 22 or less. That is 3.04 percent.

The rare cells, the ones on the low side of the frequency hole, already sit in one column. That placement was not allowed to explain the wait. Shuffles that keep every low-side cell inside one column still have a longer wait: only 199 of 10,000 are as short as 22. The column does not produce the steady introductions.

The book's solved exercise is the other check. Its longest wait is 8, and 429 of 10,000 shuffles of its own cells are that short or shorter. The same measure flags the worked example. The challenge is orderly in a way the solved Polybius is also orderly. That is not a key, and it is not a reading.
