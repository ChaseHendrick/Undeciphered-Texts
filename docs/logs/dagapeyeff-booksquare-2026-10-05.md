The worked Polybius exercise earlier in the same book has a public plaintext. Aligning its pairs with that plaintext rebuilds the square it used. If the challenge reused that square, the cells would become letters with no search, and no transposition could change their counts.

The exercise as transcribed here has 89 pairs for 92 plaintext letters. Three letters have no pair: the E of BOMBER, the A of SQUADRONS and the F of OF. One pair stands for the wrong letter: the third letter of AREA is written with the pair for Y. The alignment skips a letter only when the next two pairs agree after the skip. It does not learn from the wrong pair. That leaves 22 of 25 cells known. The three unused cells take G, X and Z in every order.
