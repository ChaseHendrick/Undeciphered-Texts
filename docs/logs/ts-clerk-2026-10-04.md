# Truppenschlüssel, from the clerk's side, 4 October 2026

Not solved. No square was searched. No plaintext is claimed.

The people who sent these were radio clerks. The useful question is what a clerk repeats and what a clerk mis-copies. It is not whether a subtler cipher is hiding underneath.

## Same desk

Station 4fc sent Nr. 129 and Nr. 130 on 8 July, both under IASRZ. After that label, both messages start `EDSTB`. Same indicator, same opening group.

Station f8y sent Nr. 109 and Nr. 110 on 6 July, sixteen minutes apart by the form times 2232 and 2248. The openings differ, `EGDOK` and `WSWGG`. The last eight letters are the same, `LMOTIYIZ`. A clerk can reuse a closing, a name or a sign-off, without reusing the first sentence.

## Not one message heard twice

Nr. 96 and Nr. 97 are both headed 1735 on 4 July. Laid side by side, 33 letters agree, 20 are a different letter, and 4 slots are a gap. Someone copying one transmission again would not change 20 letters. The likeness is at the ends of the habit, not through the whole text. The first 10 letters of the two bodies agree in 8 places: `RQMYQFWMTQ` against `RQMXQSWMTQ`. The tail agrees. The middle does not. That is two messages on the same form, with a shared opening and a shared closing.

Nr. 97 prints `RQMX-`. One letter in that group was not read. The dash stays a hole.

## The group that makes HOHOX five letters long

Nr. 110's form says 50 letters. The page prints 55, eleven groups: `HOHOX WSWGG MSHYL HNOAN GTVVT PNZPF ZKERO QNXRR OQMXS RGLMO TIYIZ`. An extra group is the ordinary copying mistake. Dropping any one of the eleven leaves 1 or 2 repeated pairs. Random letters of that length still reach 2 repeated pairs in 26 of 400 draws, and 1 repeated pair in 148 of 400. No deletion makes this message repeat pairs the way the longer ones do. Five letters is an odd length, so deleting a group also shifts every pair after it. The surplus ink is real. It is not a correction that opens the message.

## What this is not

A repeated opening is not the text of the message. A shared closing is not a signature we can read. The earlier pair-grid note still stands, and this one does not replace it. `solved` stays false and `claimed_plaintext` stays null.
