# The 13 common symbols look uniform, 5 October 2026

Outside the five rare symbols, every cell is one of 13 symbols. Their counts are 20, 17, 17, 17, 17, 16, 15, 14, 12, 12, 11, 11 and 9, which is 188 cells. In English the most common letter is about three times as common as the thirteenth, and a one-to-one substitution keeps that skew.

`engine.dagapeyeff_body` measures how far the 13 largest counts of a text are from equal, as a chi-square against their own mean. The cells score 8.66. Twenty thousand draws of 188 symbols from 13 equally likely ones have a median of 11.29, and 5,274 of them are as flat or flatter, so the cells' common body is ordinary for equal odds. Of 479,051 real 196-letter windows of the public training file, 194 are as flat, about 1 in 2,500, and the median window scores 30.25.

So the body is what equal odds give, and what real prose gives only rarely. That fits a layer that flattens letters, a plaintext that is not ordinary letters, such as numbers, or a hand-made tally like the one van Eykelen reconstructed. It does not say which. A transposition cannot change counts.
