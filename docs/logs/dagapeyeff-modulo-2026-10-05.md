# The row digit does not track the column

5 October 2026. 100,000 shuffles for the diagonal. 20,000 for the table. No letter string is stored.

Twenty alignments were scored together. Each alignment takes one digit of the cell, either axis of the 14 by 14 grid, and one of five phases. A hit means the digit index equals that position plus the phase, modulo 5. The score is the best of the twenty, so the phase was not picked after the fact.

The best is the row digit against the column, with no phase shift. It hits 56 cells. 975 of 20,000 shuffles reach 56, and 4,978 of 100,000 do. Both sit just under 5 percent.

That is only the diagonal of the table. The same four digit-and-axis pairs, scored as whole tables, have chi-square 19.5404. 12,780 of 20,000 shuffles match it. The table is ordinary, so the diagonal is not a finding.

The solved exercise, laid on the same width, reaches its own best in 1,488 of 20,000 shuffles. It does not clear either.
