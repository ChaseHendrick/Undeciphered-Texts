# A thousand attacks

4 October 2026. 1,000,000 scores. No letter string is stored.

The thousand attacks were fixed before the shuffles were drawn. Five hundred of them stir the order of the cells. Five hundred re-pair the two digits and keep the totals. A hit is a score that none of those copies reached. Looking a thousand times will stumble into a few hits even when nothing is there, so a hit is information only when the same fact shows up in a whole family, or when it is one of the two facts already measured.

Stirring the order produced 11 hits. All 11 are the same fact: the rare cells sit in one column. It shows at width 7, width 14, and width 28, which is that column, its half, and its double. It shows once the count cutoff includes cells that appear twice, not when only the singletons are counted. No lag from 1 to 100 hit. No window, no row-column correlation, no regular deletion, and none of the 99 repeating shifts hit. The plain neighbor-repeat rate was reached by 100 of the 500 stirred copies.

Re-pairing the digits produced 60 hits. One is the pairing score itself, 0 of 500. One is the correlation of the two digits inside a cell. Three are pile scores. The other 55 are windows that contain fewer different cells than a re-paired text. Re-pairing invents new cells, so a window looks more varied after it. That is the glue again, seen through a window, not a second message. No lag, no deletion, and no repeating shift hit this null either.

The battery sees the column of rare cells, and it sees that the two digits inside a cell are not paired at random. It does not see an order that a shuffle cannot explain. It does not name a letter.

No letter string is stored.
