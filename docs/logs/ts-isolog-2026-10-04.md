# Truppenschlüssel repeat slide, 4 October 2026

Not solved. No square was built. No plaintext is claimed.

This cipher swaps letter pairs, and the swap does not depend on where the pair sits. So inside a guessed phrase, two plaintext pairs are the same word-piece only when the two ciphertext pairs are the same. The slide tries twelve even-length formulae a clerk might write. It steps two letters at a time, starting on the first printed letter. A window survives when the repeats match in both directions.

Most of the formulae never repeat a pair. On a stretch of ciphertext that also never repeats a pair, every such formula survives. That is not evidence. On IASRZ Nr. 129, `KOMMANDANT` survives 35 of the 41 even windows, and none of those 35 is informative. The same pattern holds for the other formulae that do not repeat a pair.

`UNTERKUNFT` does repeat a pair: the first pair and the fourth pair are both `UN`. Only three windows on the 90-letter IASRZ message have that shape, at offsets 40, 60, and 64. Any other ten-letter formula with the same shape fits those same three windows. The word is not identified. The shorter IASRZ message and DSZPZ have no window for it at all.

The two IASRZ messages were also required to agree with each other. A placement on one and a placement on the other may not assign one ciphertext pair to two different plaintext pairs. Across all twelve formulae, the number of agreeing pairs of placements is 0.

A control encrypts `XXXXKOMMANDANTXXXXYYYY` with known squares. The slide finds the planted word at offset 4. The sieve can see a real repeat. It does not see one that ties these three messages together.

`solved` stays false and `claimed_plaintext` stays null.
