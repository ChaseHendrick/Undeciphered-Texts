**In plain words.** Rongorongo is a script of small carved figures found on about two dozen wooden objects from Rapa Nui. Lines alternate direction, so the tablet is turned upside down at the end of each line. A 2024 study dated the wood of one tablet to before European contact, but the wood could have been carved later. We have not proposed a reading. The plan is first to keep originals, plaster casts and photos of the same object from being counted as separate texts.

These are questions about the material that can be measured before anyone proposes a reading. Answering all of them would not decipher the script; it would make a later claim testable. Nothing here is a reading, and no probability of decipherment is assigned.

Ferrara and colleagues' 2024 primary study dates wood from four tablets and reports one notably older specimen. Crucially, it discusses reused wood and explicitly allows engraving later than the material's age. Radiocarbon evidence alone therefore does not date the sign carving precisely or decipher it. The authors' broader origins interpretation should remain distinct from their material measurements. [Published study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53063-7), [official full-text XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10837134/fullTextXML).

## Corpus and provenance

The British Museum's authored record for wooden tablet Oc1903,-.150 describes alternating line orientation. Its older origins and reading proposals are attributed museum interpretations, not consensus established by this note. A separate museum record, Oc1981,Q.1642, is a **plaster cast**, not another original inscribed wooden text. Do not inflate the corpus by counting the original, its cast and published photographs as independent inscriptions. [Original tablet record](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/wooden-tablet-with-rongorongo-inscription/YQFZoZpx2-hP1Q?hl=en), [cast catalog](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Oc1981-Q-1642).

The original-tablet description was accessible through the museum's Google Arts & Culture publication. Direct museum collection extraction returned 403. The cast identification was available in the indexed museum record; its full page was blocked. Publisher and PMC article views were initially unavailable, but the official Europe PMC API supplied the authored article XML. No complete sign corpus or new object photographs were acquired here.

## Proposed next experiment, not executed here

1. Make an object ledger with custodian IDs, original/cast/copy flags, sides, line starts, rotation and transcription versions. Limit an initial annotation trial to four documented originals. Establish image-use and cultural conditions before reuse.
2. Preserve compound or ligature readings and doubtful signs. Two annotators should align at most 200 sign occurrences without seeing a proposed translation. Publish disagreement and missing-material counts.
3. Search predeclared repeated sign sequences of lengths 2 through 8, with at most 10,000 pair comparisons and 100 saved alignments. Reserve one entire original tablet before choosing normalization or sequence length. Compare results with seeded within-line permutations preserving line lengths.

The useful result is an auditable parallel-passage or annotation analysis, with dependence and orientation controlled. It does not supply a phonetic value or ritual meaning.

## Evidence standard

Any reading must keep its rules on independent tablets and obtain linguistic grounding outside the passages used to invent them. A proposed calendar, chant or creation narrative needs separate supporting evidence, not a caption treated as plaintext. Material chronology, writing-system classification and semantic decipherment are different questions.

The next trigger is an independently checked transcription, object imaging release or grounded reading with frozen unseen predictions. A claim that old wood proves pre-contact writing is stronger than the cited measurements establish on their own.
