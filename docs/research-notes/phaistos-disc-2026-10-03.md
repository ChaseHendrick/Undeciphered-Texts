Source check: **2026-10-03**. Category: undeciphered inscription; no language or cipher family is established here.

Heraklion Archaeological Museum's description records 241 stamped signs in 61 groups on two sides, read from the edge inward. It treats the groups as presumed words and mentions a hymn or incantation interpretation, while stating that no definitive conclusion has been reached about the contents or relationship to other Aegean scripts. Those interpretations are not recovered plaintext. [Custodian description](https://heraklionmuseum.gr/en/exhibit/the-phaistos-disc/), [collection record](https://ca.heraklionmuseum.gr/ca/pawtucket/index.php/Detail/objects/336).

## Corpus and access

The museum page was retrieved as HTML after the web extraction tool failed. Its description was inspected; no fresh sign transcription was made from an original or photograph. The collection record's indexed description identifies the same object, but direct extraction timed out. These are two descriptions of **one** artifact, not two independent texts.

Use the custodian's declared count when describing that source. If a transcription has a different count, keep both counts with an explicit location-by-location explanation. Do not remove a doubtful impression merely to make the numbers agree. A sign inventory, side numbering, group separators and reading direction must be independently declared; Roman transliteration symbols are identifiers, not known phonetic values.

## Proposed next experiment, not executed here

1. Obtain two independently prepared, cited sign annotations with image access permitted by the custodian. Reconcile at most the 241 impressions in the museum count, preserving discrepancies, side/group IDs and uncertain sign identities.
2. Freeze no more than 12 explicitly justified annotation or grouping variants before any language scoring. Compare repeated-group and sign-position statistics across variants, using at most 2,000 seeded within-side permutations per predeclared statistic.
3. Report how much the results depend on each disputed sign or boundary. A leave-group-out prediction is only an internal consistency check because all groups come from the same artifact; do not call it independent historical validation.

This is a transcription and ambiguity experiment, not a search for a hymn whose words can be arranged to fit.

## Evidence standard and tractability

The lack of a second independently contextualized text in this proposed dataset limits how strongly a fitted reading can be tested. Repeated groups are useful observations, but selecting sign values and a language after inspecting all groups risks overfitting. A proposed reading should specify every sign and exception, work without reassigning values, and gain support from independent linguistic or archaeological evidence.

The immediate useful output is a transparent sign crosswalk and uncertainty report. No statistical fit is labeled a decipherment, and no confidence score based on the A-Z engine is assigned. A new independently contextualized inscription would change what can be tested more substantially than another unconstrained phrase search.
