**In plain words.** Linear A was used on Minoan Crete from about 1800 to 1450 BC, mostly on clay tablets for keeping accounts. Many of its signs look like signs of the later Linear B, which was deciphered as Greek in 1952, so scholars can pronounce Linear A roughly. But the language behind it is unknown, so the words cannot be understood. We have not proposed a reading. The plan is to test which repeated sign sequences hold up across different find sites.

These are questions about the material that can be measured before anyone proposes a reading. Answering all of them would not decipher the script; it would make a later claim testable. Nothing here is a reading, and no probability of decipherment is assigned.

## Corpus and limits

The live project is a research entry point, not a certificate that every known inscription is currently included. Its initial paper describes a database beginning with administrative tablets and a plan for wider coverage. It distinguishes the GORILA printed corpus's physical-layout drawings from functional arrangements, and documents uncertain readings, unclassified signs and context-dependent sign functions. Verify the included document list when taking a snapshot; do not inherit a corpus size from an older paper as today's count. [Database design and coverage](https://sigla.phis.me/paper.html).

Use inscription IDs, find-place, support type, side/line position, sign classification numbers, attested shapes and uncertainty. Preserve a possible syllabic/logographic function as an annotation with its source, not a forced semantic label. The current interface and the paper were inspected; a complete export was not acquired or analyzed in this pass.

## Proposed next experiment, not executed here

1. Select at most 300 accessible documents from a versioned export whose reuse terms and coverage are recorded. Check 30 against displayed sign occurrences with two independent annotators. Retain disagreements as alternative inventories.
2. Compare canonical sign numbers with a second inventory that preserves declared graphic variants. Predeclare n-gram orders 1 through 3 and at most 2,000 seeded, document-length-preserving permutations for each statistic. Count documents and distinct find-places as well as token occurrences.
3. Reserve a find-place containing enough eligible documents before feature selection. Evaluate repeated-sequence predictions separately for administrative and non-administrative supports. Group related or duplicate objects across splits. Report insufficient test coverage rather than filling it with training documents.

A useful result could identify a robust distributional pattern, an apparent allograph distinction or an annotation disagreement. Such a result would remain a corpus observation, not a decipherment.

## Evidence standard

Test a proposed reading on unseen inscriptions without changing sign values or inventing missing words. Distinguish conventional Linear B-derived sounds, inferred document function and a proposed language. Require independently supported linguistic correspondences and archaeological context, covering counterexamples as well as favorable matches. An English language score or one possible place name is inadequate.

Linear B is a solved comparative control discussed by the source, not a license to force Linear A into Greek. The main near-term opportunity is auditable sign/context comparison; no numerical probability of solving the language is assigned.
