# How progress on unread scripts actually happens

This is a methods note for `landscape.md` and `closest.md`. It describes the historical pattern and the working rules. It does not give readings of undeciphered texts.

The short version: scripts fall when someone can **predict** a feature they did not use to build the hypothesis. They do not fall when someone finds a language family with some similar-looking words.

---

## 1. Two problems that get called one word

**Script decipherment** maps signs to sounds or to words (a syllabary, an alphabet, a logosyllabary). After Griffith (1911), a Meroitic inscription can be transliterated by a student. That job is done.

**Language decipherment** maps those readings to meanings and grammar. Meroitic, Etruscan, and northeastern Iberian are stuck here, with very different amounts of grammar already known.

A third problem is prior and duller: **is this writing?** Vinča marks, the Dispilio plank, Pictish symbols, and (for some scholars) Indus seals and rongorongo tablets may encode identity, ritual, or memory without encoding speech. Statistics can show non-randomness. Non-randomness is also produced by calendars, tallies, and decoration. Farmer, Sproat, and Witzel’s 2004 critique of Indus, and Routledge’s ethnographic description of rongorongo, are reminders to ask this before building a phonetic grid.

Unicode encoding is none of the three. The Cypro-Minoan proposal says the repertoire is a catalogue so that debate about values can be digitized: https://www.unicode.org/wg2/docs/n5135-cyprominoan.pdf

## 2. The prediction standard

Linear B is the model because the order of operations was right.

- Bennett and others stabilized the **sign list**.
- Kober found **inflection**: groups of signs that alternated in a way that looked like prefixes or suffixes, and she refused to assign sounds prematurely.
- Ventris built a **grid** (same vowel down a column, same consonant along a row) from those alternations. Cambridge reconstruction of the process: https://www.classics.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/process.pdf
- Only then did he test a language. He had expected Etruscan. The grid forced Greek. Work Note 20 is dated 1 June 1952; the BBC announcement is 1 July 1952. https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/cracking-the-code-the-decipherment-of-linear-b-60-years-on
- The check was **external to the grid**: Greek words for vessels and numbers matched the ideograms drawn beside them (the Pylos tablet series Ta, especially the tripod tablet). If the language guess had been wrong, the pictures would have contradicted the readings.

That last step is the one almost every modern “decipherment” skips. Fitting a wordlist to a transliteration you are free to adjust is not a test. A test is:

- values fixed in public,
- then applied to a text, a sign, or a grammatical slot that was not part of the fit,
- by someone who would prefer the hypothesis to fail.

Isthmian is the cautionary version of the same design. Justeson and Kaufman’s 1993 *Science* paper stated a language (pre-proto-Zoquean) and readings. A newly recovered column was later offered as confirmation. Houston and Coe offered a mask text as disconfirmation. The field did not “almost decipher” Isthmian by averaging those papers. It acquired a dispute with teeth. That is better than a dispute without teeth. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.259.5102.1703

Linear Elamite in 2022 is in the same procedural position as Isthmian in 1993: a published grid, some bilingual proper names, and critics who have not signed on. The next move is a held-out inscription, not a documentary.

## 3. What actually unlocked scripts (patterns, not romance)

| Case | What did the work | What did not |
|---|---|---|
| Egyptian hieroglyphs | Rosetta Stone (Greek parallel), cartouches as names, Coptic as the later form of the language. Champollion 1822 | Treating every sign as a pure idea-picture |
| Old Persian cuneiform | Known royal names and a repeating formula (“X, great king, king of kings…”), then Rawlinson’s longer Bisotun text | Waiting for a bilingual of the whole inscription |
| Ugaritic | About 30 signs, so an alphabet; Semitic language guess; names and word patterns confirmed within months (1930–31) | Corpus size. The tablets are long, but the *script type* was what made speed possible |
| Cypriot syllabary | Phoenician–Greek bilingual (Idalion) plus Greek as the target | Comparison with Linear A, which came later and did not drive the reading |
| Maya | Knorozov’s argument that signs are phonetic as well as logographic; Landa’s “alphabet” used critically, not literally; Proskouriakoff showing that Classic texts are history; modern Mayan languages as the target | Any single “Rosetta” monument |
| Anatolian hieroglyphs | Seals and texts that also exist in Hittite cuneiform; Luwian as the language | Pure pictographic guessing |
| Carian | Egyptian bilinguals and names, late 20th century (Adiego, Schürr, and others) | The assumption that a Greek-looking alphabet must write Greek |
| Meroitic script | Egyptian spellings of Kushite kings. Griffith 1909–1911 | A literary bilingual, which still does not exist |
| Linear B | Internal grid, then Greek, then ideogram check | Ventris’s original Etruscan hunch |
| NE Iberian | Coin legends: the same name in Iberian and in Latin or Greek. Gómez-Moreno | A theory of Basque |

The pattern is boring. You need at least one of:

- a parallel text in a known language,
- a script type small enough to brute-force (alphabet, simple syllabary) **and** a constrained language family,
- or a very large internal structure (inflection) plus an external check (pictures, tallies, calendars, names).

You do not get a logosyllabic decipherment from a short monolingual corpus and a list of candidate languages. That is the shape of Linear A, Indus, Byblos, Phaistos, and rongorongo.

## 4. Partial knowledge is real and it is not a consolation prize

Serious fields already hold ground that popular accounts call “still a total mystery.”

**Numerals and measures.** Proto-Elamite shares the logic of proto-cuneiform accounts: sexagesimal counts, a decimal system for people and animals, a bisexagesimal system, a capacity system for grain. Totals on the tablet check the reading of the numbers even when the commodity word is unknown. Englund: https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/Preprints/P183.PDF . This is a decipherment of a subsystem.

**Ideograms by context.** Linear A grain, fig, oil, wine, livestock, and person signs are studied by their distribution and by resemblance to Linear B formats (Palmer, summarized in Petrolito et al. 2015: https://aclanthology.org/W15-3715.pdf). That does not pronounce the Minoan word for grain.

**Calendars.** The Mamari rongorongo sequence matches a lunar month. Zapotec and Isthmian day-counts use a Mesoamerican calendar that is known from other traditions. These are identifications of **function**, and they do not deliver the surrounding sentences.

**Formulae.** Etruscan epitaphs, Meroitic funerary sentences, and Iberian ownership or dedication frames repeat. Unknown words in a known frame are how lexicons grow. The frame is the method.

**Proper names.** Pyrgi, Idalion, coin legends, Egyptian renderings of Meroitic kings, Akkadian renderings of Elamite names. Names are dangerous if they are the only evidence (too many ways to slice them) and decisive if the same name is spelled in two scripts.

**Daughter scripts.** Linear B does not decipher Linear A, but it licenses a hypothesis about a minority of shared signs. The Cypriot syllabary does the same, more weakly, for Cypro-Minoan. Mnamon’s statement of how few Linear A signs survive a strict homophone test is the model of intellectual honesty: https://mnamon.sns.it/index.php?id=19&lang=en&page=Scrittura

## 5. Editions before algorithms

Every corpus in this set has been “solved” wrongly by someone who did not cite a sign number.

The order that produces progress:

1. **Images** tied to museum numbers (CDLI, Beinecke, SigLA, Hesperia, REM, CISI photographs).
2. **A sign list with variants**, in which joining two signs or splitting one is an explicit edit. Mahadevan 417 vs Wells ~700 is not a minor metadata issue; it changes every n-gram study of Indus. Barthel vs Fischer does the same for rongorongo. Olivier vs later Cypro-Minoan lists does the same.
3. **A diplomatic transcription** that marks damage, overstrike, and uncertain signs. Proto-Elamite and Linear A are full of these. A clean string of tokens hides the uncertainty computational papers then treat as fact.
4. **A concordance** (sign in context). This is what Younger’s Linear A site, Mahadevan’s tables, Barthel’s *Grundlagen*, and REM were for.
5. Only then a **hypothesis** with a fixed mapping.
6. Only then a **prediction**.

Computational clustering is part of step 2, not a replacement for steps 5–6. Useful examples that know their place:

- Proto-Elamite sign and topic clustering as corpus description, not translation: https://aclanthology.org/W19-2516.pdf
- Numeral-system disambiguation: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00090v1
- Rongorongo n-gram tests of an already stated syllabic idea: https://aclanthology.org/2026.lt4hala-1.16.pdf
- Voynich topic and word-structure studies (Bowern and Lindemann 2021).

Harmful pattern, visible in Linear A and Indus papers every year: take a conventional transliteration, search a multilingual dictionary, report the best hits, call it evidence of Hurrian or Dravidian or Sanskrit. With short words and a free choice of language, hits are guaranteed. The 2024 MDPI Linear A paper is unusually careful in calling its matches inconclusive: https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/15/2/73 . Most are not.

## 6. Why short logosyllabic corpora stall

A syllabary of 80–100 signs needs a lot of repeated words with inflection or a bilingual. Linear B had the repetitions. Linear A has about 7,400 signs spread over 1,400 objects, many of them one to five signs long (Corazza’s discussion of signs-per-document: https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/90a2e16b-e280-42ac-92f1-a9961ef98ca6/d-2-402-corazza-computational-methods.pdf). Cypro-Minoan is worse: on the order of 21 inscriptions longer than ten signs.

Indus is the extreme case. Five-sign texts do not contain enough syntax to identify a language, and the same five signs can be parsed as logograms, syllables, or a non-linguistic code. Adding the 3,000th seal of length five improves the **frequency table** and does not create a sentence.

One-text scripts (Phaistos, Cascajal, the Singapore fragment, the Dispilio plank) are not “awaiting the right scholar.” The data required for a unique mapping is not in the object. Phaistos has 45 types and no second monument (https://anetoday.org/phaistos-disk/).

Length is not enough either. The Voynich manuscript has tens of thousands of word tokens and still no solution, because there is no external anchor: no bilingual, no agreed plant identifications, no numeral system that checks itself, and a word grammar odd enough that both “unknown language” and “generated pseudo-text” remain possible. https://voynich.nu/a4_word.html

## 7. Language identification is a separate failure mode

Once a script is readable, family arguments still go wrong. The disciplined version:

- Compare **morphology and regular sound correspondences**, not isolated lookalikes.
- Prefer explanations that predict a form in a text you have not glossed.
- State rival hypotheses. Meroitic is the model: Rilly’s East Sudanic syntax argument and Rowan’s Afroasiatic phonotactic argument are both public, and the secure wordlist is small enough that neither side gets to invent the rest. https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3128r3sw
- Do not upgrade a typological similarity (“agglutinative, therefore X”) into a classification. Iberian is agglutinative and not thereby Basque.

Etruscan shows the ceiling. The Tyrsenian link to Raetic and Lemnian is accepted as a working hypothesis because the shared items are grammatical and few, not because someone translated the Liber Linteus into Lemnian.

## 8. Forgeries, circular drawings, and damaged wood

Epigraphy fails before linguistics when the picture is wrong.

- Several rongorongo objects were cut with steel or made for sale. Fischer and the older literature flag them; using them in a training set poisons frequencies. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongorongo
- Barthel’s drawings and Fischer’s drawings disagree on real lines. A model trained on one is not a reading of the wood.
- The Phaistos Disc’s authenticity is supported by parallels discovered after 1908, and it has still never been thermoluminescence-dated. Both facts belong in the data sheet.
- Cascajal was not excavated scientifically. Later lab work on the stone can support authenticity and still leave the provenance weaker than a stratified tablet. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1131492
- Radiocarbon dates the **support**. The 2024 rongorongo dates (one tablet’s wood in 1493–1509, three later) are about the history of writing on Rapa Nui, not about glyph values. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7
- The Dispilio date is a Neolithic lakeshore date. The paper does not claim a decipherment. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/radiocarbon-dating-of-the-neolithic-lakeside-settlement-of-dispilio-kastoria-northern-greece/759AA29502776E142883F1971293BEB1

## 9. Working rules for anyone adding to this directory

1. Separate **edition**, **sign list**, **hypothesis**, and **translation** into different documents. Mixing them is how false readings spread.
2. Never publish a translation of Linear A, Indus, rongorongo, Phaistos, Byblos, Cascajal, Voynich, or Rohonc as a result. Publish a mapping, then a prediction, then stop.
3. Conventional transliterations are not evidence. Label Linear A B-values as conventional. Label Meroitic and Etruscan transliterations as real readings of the script.
4. Count hapaxes. A sign or word that occurs once cannot define a value.
5. Report text-length histograms before any language claim. If the median text is under six signs, say so in the first paragraph.
6. Keep a **claim ledger**: author, year, language proposed, what would falsify it, whether anyone tried. Retire claims that failed. Do not “ensemble” them.
7. Prefer open images and museum numbers (CDLI, Beinecke, Hesperia, SigLA, Persée REM, Roots Singapore Stone) over a CSV of unknown provenance. GitHub corpora of Indus or Linear A are derivative and often silently normalize variants.
8. If a method would also “decipher” a scrambled control text, it has not deciphered this one. Build the control.
9. Calendars, numerals, and repeated formulae are the places to work. Unique mythological translations are where cranks work.
10. A prize (Indus, 2025) is not peer review.

## 10. What “done” will look like, by case

- **Meroitic / Etruscan / Iberian:** a dictionary entry with a formula citation and a rival gloss that lost. No press conference.
- **Linear Elamite:** a critic who dislikes the 2022 paper nevertheless reads a disputed inscription the same way, or a specific value is withdrawn.
- **Cypro-Minoan or Linear A:** a grid published before a new long tablet is read, which then matches names or tallies on that tablet. Until a new tablet exists, “done” will not happen.
- **Proto-Elamite:** more commodity identifications that survive the totals, still without a spoken language.
- **Indus:** not a full decipherment. The honest finish, absent a bilingual, is a stable sign list and a syntax of positions.
- **Rongorongo:** either a demonstration that the parallels are linguistic (same structure as a known genre, predicted on a tablet not used to build the mapping) or a clear argument that they are mnemonic. Both would be progress. A syllabus of “all glyphs mean X” will not.
- **Phaistos, Cascajal, Dispilio, Tărtăria:** no finish is available. Stop expecting one.
- **Voynich:** a mapping that reads labels and paragraphs in one system and tells a conservator something new and true about a drawing. Entropy will not be that mapping.
