# K4 as period 19 Quagmire I, II and IV, with an English search, 18 October 2026

`solved` is false and `claimed_plaintext` is null. Every K4 decryption below is an unverified search output and, by the rule set before the run, noise.

## Question

`engine.k4_sat` found that in-place periodic Quagmire I, II and IV at period 19 can place both clues (EASTNORTHEAST at 21, BERLINCLOCK at 63). Random strings fit about 2 to 4 percent of the time, which after 208 family and period tests is not significant. The clues alone cannot go further. The question here is whether a language search can, and whether that search has the power to find a period 19 Quagmire key on 97 letters at all.

## Method

Code: `engine/k4_quagmire19.py` (driver) and `engine/k4_quagmire19.c` (kernel, compiled on first use as in `engine/k4_columnar_bnb.py`).

Families, letters 0 to 25, K = K[i mod 19]:

- Quagmire I: c = rank_A(p) + K (keyed plain alphabet, straight cipher alphabet).
- Quagmire II: rank_B(c) = p + K (straight plain, keyed cipher alphabet).
- Quagmire IV: rank_B(c) = rank_A(p) + K (both keyed).

State: the keyed alphabet or alphabets as full permutations and the 19 shifts. 15 of the 19 columns hold at least one clue letter. Their shifts are not free: each column takes the shift that most of its clue letters ask for under the current alphabets. The other 4 shifts are free state.

Clues are a penalty, not a hard rule. Each clue letter still wrong costs lambda nats. The main runs use lambda = 5. A forced-fit pass on Quagmire I uses lambda = 30. The solver also keeps the best state with every clue letter right ("clue-feasible"), and that is the state reported when one exists.

Objective: English log probability of the whole 97-letter decryption from `engine.language.get_model()` (first three letters from the shorter contexts, then quadgrams), minus the penalty. The full table is exported to C.

Moves: swap two letters of one alphabet (75 percent of moves), change a free shift (15 percent), and shift a group of clue letters that are tied by shared columns along the alphabet with their spacing kept (10 percent). A rotation of a whole alphabet is a free gauge here, so it is not a move. Annealing is geometric from T = 6 to T = 0.3 over 1,000,000 moves, with 6 restarts from random alphabets: 6 million evaluations, about 5 seconds per run on one core (about 1.2 million evaluations per second).

The same budget was used for every planted text, every null and K4.

## Power on planted English

Held-out text: `engine/data/neural_heldout_doyle.txt` and `engine/data/neural_audit_wells.txt`, cut into disjoint 97-letter windows. Ten windows were drawn, both clues were written in at 21 and 63, and each was enciphered under each family with a random period 19 key and either a random permutation alphabet or a keyword alphabet (KRYPTOS style: keyword letters once, then the rest in order) from a random 6 to 10 letter word of the held-out text. Quagmire IV got two independent alphabets of the same kind. The solver ran blind. The true decryptions score -233.8 to -189.1 nats (median -210.9).

24 of the 97 letters are clue letters, so about 25 percent right is what the clues give for free.

Quagmire I has real power. Where it misses exactness, the found decryption almost always scores at or above the true one, so the misses are a few letters the English model prefers otherwise, not search failure. One text (random alphabet, text 1) was a search failure.

Quagmire II has low power and Quagmire IV has none at this budget. In the Quagmire IV runs the solver never reached a clue-feasible state, and its best states score 42 to 88 nats below the true key, so this is search failure, not a model that prefers wrong text.

## What was tried to raise Quagmire II and IV power

Benchmarks on 1 to 3 planted texts per family, random alphabets, before the main run:

- Temperature and penalty: lambda 2 to 20, T from 2 to 15 down to 0.15 to 1.5. Quagmire II and IV best states had 29 to 47 letters right and scored 40 to 130 nats under the true key. One setting (lambda 4, no group shifts) recovered one Quagmire II text exactly in one restart of four, but on a six-text bench (three II, three IV) it recovered 1 of 6 at 4 by 1,000,000 moves and 1 of 6 at one anneal of 8,000,000 moves.
- Longer anneals (one restart of 16,000,000 moves): Quagmire II 37 and 44 letters right.
- Parallel tempering (6 to 12 replicas, 4,000,000 to 16,000,000 moves): Quagmire II 36 to 47, Quagmire IV 29 to 35 letters right.
- Heat-bath moves (all 25 swaps of one letter weighed at once): Quagmire II 33 to 36, Quagmire IV 34 to 36.
- All 19 shifts as free state with the clue penalty, instead of derived: worse (Quagmire II 19 to 32).
- Large moves (move one clue letter, then hill-climb the non-clue letters for 150 to 500 steps): worse on the six-text bench (22 to 40 letters).
- Group shifts of tied clue letters: no clear effect either way.

Two oracle diagnostics on one Quagmire II text show where the difficulty is. With the true shifts given and the alphabet random, the solver recovered the text exactly in 4 of 4 restarts of 1,000,000 moves. With the 9 cipher letters tied by shared clue columns placed right and the other 17 scrambled, it recovered the text in 0 of 4 runs; with 10 scrambled, 2 of 4; with 3 to 6 scrambled, nearly always. So the obstacle is the shifts of the columns with one clue letter or none, which hang on letters that no clue ties down. A SAT solution of the clue constraints fixes at most what the second diagnostic gave for free, so seeding from SAT solutions was not run: the diagnostic already shows it would not be enough.

None of the improvements worked. The main run uses plain annealing with a modest share of group shifts.

It is noise.

## Timing

One core (`taskset -c 3`). Tuning and diagnostics about 11 minutes. Main run (K4, 60 planted texts, 60 nulls) 625 seconds. Forced-fit pass 110 seconds. A deterministic rerun of the K4 and null runs to record null index of coincidence gave identical scores (440 seconds). About 31 minutes in all. Tests take about 2 seconds.

## Limits

- The score is English only. A K4 plaintext in another language, or English with heavy misspelling, would not score well under this model even with the right key.
- Period 19 was chosen after looking at the K4 clues (it is one of the periods `engine.k4_sat` did not block). Any K4 result at period 19 would need a fresh held-out check, for example a hold-out clue or a test at an unseen setting, before it meant anything. None arose.
- Power is demonstrated for Quagmire I only. For Quagmire II (about 20 percent of planted texts read) and Quagmire IV (none), the absence of a K4 lead says almost nothing: this solver would likely miss the right key even if K4 were enciphered that way.
- Penalty, not hard constraint: at lambda 5 the solver may trade clue letters for English, so the lambda 5 K4 rows are not clue-exact. The lambda 30 pass covers the clue-exact case for Quagmire I.
- In-place models only. Nothing here speaks to transposition before or after the substitution.
- Planted texts are 97-letter windows of two held-out English sources with the clues written in, so their English score is a little lower than plain prose.
