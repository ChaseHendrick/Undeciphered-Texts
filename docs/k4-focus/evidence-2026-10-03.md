# K4 technical evidence audit, 3 October 2026

**No new plaintext or historical solution is claimed.** This audit freezes the public input and distinguishes a reference answer, a recovery method and the meaning of the recovered message. The verifier's implementation and answer custody were not independently audited here. An accepted text would establish a reference comparison, not demonstrate the historical cipher. ## Attributable construction statements

Scheidt's edited 2005 interview identifies English and a masking step, says that step may be unfamiliar, and acknowledges uncertainty about Sanborn's final changes. It does not specify a key schedule or reversible formula. [Direct Scheidt interview](https://www.wired.com/2005/01/inside-info-on-kryptos-codes/).

Sanborn's separate 2005 interview describes modifying Scheidt's systems and developing his own. He also distinguishes decoded text from its wider interpretation. This supports caution about treating a familiar family as confirmed K4 construction. [Direct Sanborn interview](https://www.wired.com/2005/01/questions-for-kryptos-creator/).

The 2007 PBS transcript discusses substitution and transposition and attributes masking to Scheidt. Vowel removal and phonetic spelling are presenter examples, and prior obfuscation is Gillogly's conjecture. They are not an artist-confirmed recipe. [PBS transcript](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/transcripts/3411_sciencen.html).

First-hand coverage of Sanborn's 12 November 2025 museum talk identifies the Berlin **World Clock**, mentions his 1986 Egypt trip and the 1989 Berlin Wall fall, and associates the work with delivering a message. Its K5 connection is thematic. These clues supply no additional aligned word, clock-to-letter mapping, reference time or encryption equation. The linked original open-letter scan returned HTTP 403, so that document was not examined. [First-hand talk coverage](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/cia-kryptos-puzzle-creator-releases-final-clues/), [unavailable linked letter](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/26229389-adobe-scan-nov-12-2025/).

The older CLOCK article discusses several Berlin clocks, including a Binninger clock, without establishing it as the required mechanism. The newer identification makes World Clock the supported referent; the older interpretation must not silently supply a keystream. [2014 interview](https://www.wired.com/2014/11/second-kryptos-clue/), [2025 talk coverage](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/cia-kryptos-puzzle-creator-releases-final-clues/).

The August 2025 artist letter also includes a semantic hint concerning secrecy and power. It supplies no aligned plaintext or cipher specification. The artist's current website still contains prose anticipating the November 2025 sale beside a newer custodian video link; its undated status is not treated as a fresh global assertion. [Artist letter](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/OpenLetterAug2025.html), [artist website](https://jimsanborn.net/content.html).

## Preserved discrepancies and unavailable evidence

- The 2005 Scheidt interview and 2007 PBS presentation mention 98 characters; the 2025 talk article contains a 79-letter description. These counts conflict with the independently matched 97-letter input and artist's August letter. They do not authorize inserting or deleting a character.
- RR's October account says Scheidt knows the method; Scheidt's own earlier interview does not confirm Sanborn's final construction. This audit does not resolve that discrepancy by assigning private knowledge to him.
- RR's 2025 account reports a 50-year archive seal. The Smithsonian finding aid revised on 6 May 2026 instead provides collection-level appointment and access-copy conditions. Neither statement settles the accessibility of each sensitive item. No underlying archive page was requested. [Custodian finding aid](https://sirismm.si.edu/EADpdfs/AAA.sanbojim.pdf).
- NYT's 2010 article URL carries 21 November while participant chronology lists the 20th; WIRED's contemporaneous Saturday reference supports the announcement date. The original NYT text was unavailable. The January 2020 original and mirror, and November 2025 original letter, remain recorded as unavailable rather than silently replaced by guessed quotations.

## Prioritized conditional models

This ordering measures **testability and bounded free parameters**, not the probability of a historical solution. Every model below is a researcher assumption. No checked source endorses its formula. These differ from direct Progressive Key, the finite direct Condi keyword dictionary and the earlier pure transposition portfolio. Some now overlap the [new composition experiment](model-experiments-2026-10-03.md); that overlap is explicit.

1. **Complete a selected composition space.** Combine fixed A-Z or KRYPTOS alphabets with ragged layouts and repeating, Beaufort or declared feedback streams in both layer orders. Cap periods and widths at 32 and the next run at 10,000 model checks. The newly logged sample failed all fully determined reserved-clue predictions but left untested combinations and partial keys. Finishing a predeclared subset is more informative than relabeling that sample exhaustive. Weak motivation: a masking stage and the sculpture's visible classical constructions. Missing evidence: the actual order, alphabet and route.
2. **Test physical-row resets.** Use the exact 4/31/31/31 layout with stream state either continuous or reset only at its three observed boundaries. Fix the alphabet choices in advance, periods at 1 through 16 and families to repeating, Beaufort or plaintext-autokey; cap 5,000 models. This changes the global-stream assumption without permitting arbitrary breakpoints. Missing evidence: no source says K4 resets at physical rows. Restore original plaintext coordinates and first verify hidden-key synthetic controls spanning a reset.
3. **Test a finite public-text running-key hypothesis.** Freeze K2 and K3 recovered text from the repo's cited certificates, including deliberate spelling and X separators. Try only complete 97-letter windows, both directions, two fixed alphabets and explicit Vigenere/Beaufort arithmetic; cap 5,000 checks. Publish donor hashes and offsets. Do not search unrelated books or edit donor text after a near match. Motivation is the relation among the sculpture's sections, not a claim that they provide the key. Missing evidence: no source identifies a donor or start position.
4. **Test two fixed-alphabet substitution layers.** Predeclare each layer's alphabet as A-Z or KRYPTOS, periods at 1 through 8, operations and order, with a 10,000-check and 10-second bound. Equal-alphabet repeating layers may collapse algebraically into one stream and must not be counted as independent mechanisms. Different alphabet orders permit a genuinely different mapping. Unknown keys remain symbolic; a witness fitted to disclosed letters is not a recovery. Missing evidence: no confirmed layer count, and excess key freedom can leave the reserved group wholly unknown.

A World Clock mechanism remains a deferred hypothesis. The landmark identification alone provides no defensible date, reference hour, reading route, city ordering or alphabet conversion. A finite clock test requires those choices to come from additional evidence or an openly declared arbitrary model. The 1986/1989 events are not quietly promoted to numeric keys or new crib words.

For each experiment, fit only one published group and reserve the other before selection; report determined mismatches, exact predictions and unknowns separately. Because these clues have already been used across exploratory runs, they are disclosed-position checks, not a perpetually untouched benchmark. A claim of recovery still needs the complete text, exact forward reconstruction and independent evidence. Nr. 86, private archival plaintext and reference-verifier probing are outside this audit.
